# Integration of a Wind Farm in the Electrical Grid

The Electrical Grid at a large territory level is organized in several layers, so that there is a set of elements that does the "*transport*" from the production plants (thermal, hydraulic, nuclear, combined cycle, wind, photovoltaic, etc.) to the vicinities of the consumption points. The distance between these Grid nodes can be hundreds or even thousands of kilometers. For this function very high voltage is used, with several ranges depending on distances and other criteria, but typically between 130KV and 400 KV, of three-phase grid. In some sections the connection between these grid nodes is made with direct current, with voltages above 400 KV. This part of the Grid is known as "**High Voltage Grid**" or also "**Transport Grid**"

The consumption points can be industrial, residential or large cities. The supply to large industrial clients and large cities is carried out with another grid type, called "**Medium Grid**", three-phase with voltages between 12KV and 60 KV. In large urban agglomerations the supply is carried out in Low Voltage (less than 1kV).

The objectives of this structure are:

- Minimize the transmission and distribution losses.

- Increase the controllability of the global system.

- Have sufficient flexibility to attend the consumption changes.

In the majority of countries, the High Voltage Grid management is entrusted to entities controlled by the State, while the Distribution Grids are controlled by independent companies.

Given that each wind farm has an appreciable production, from the point of view of influence on the global Grid, they generally connect directly to the High Grid.

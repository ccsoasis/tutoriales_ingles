# Corrective Maintenance

The **Corrective Maintenance** encompasses all the operations related to the repair of some of the Wind Turbine elements, or of the remaining Farm elements that break.

The elements that have more incidents of this type in the wind turbines are:

- The Blades:  
They break or crack.

- The Electrical Generator:  
It burns. Without using the slip ring contacts (in the DFIGs), there are usually wear problems.

- The Blade Pitch Mechanism:  
Those based on hydraulic cylinders can suffer jamming and supply failures.  
Those based on electrical motors can burn.  
In both cases the electronic control elements (amplifiers, controllers, etc.) can break.  
They can also

- The Gearbox:  
It can suffer different internal faults, in its gears, generally related to mechanical over-stresses in the low speed shaft (the one connected with the Hub and the Blades) and also due to abrupt changes in the high shaft (the one connected with the electrical generator)

- The Rotor Bearing:  
This element is the one that sustains the greatest mechanical stress demand, since it must support the Hub and Blades weight, and the changes derived from the wind (direction and gusts)

- The brake discs:  
Normal and extraordinary wear due to fast brakings.

- The capacitor banks:  
Some wind turbines (especially those that have a squirrel-cage generator directly connected to the Grid) incorporate capacitor banks that are used to correct the power factor delivered by each wind turbine. These banks have a certain useful life, lower in those that do not use thyristor connection.

Some of these operations entail the use of elements external to the Farm (for example cranes) which makes them greatly more expensive.

# What is a Wind Farm?

We can describe a Wind Farm as an Electrical Energy Production installation from the wind, composed of several Wind Turbines and a set of auxiliary elements.

They generally export their production to the National Electrical Distribution System, although there are also private installations in which they supply directly to other Industrial Plants or Clients, large electricity consumers.

Its main elements are:

- The **Wind Turbines**

- The **power cabling system** that connects these Wind Turbines with the Common Connection Point (**PCC**)

- The **Electrical Substation**, which connects the Common Connection Point (PCC) with the external Electrical Grid.

- The **Monitoring and Control System (SCADA)**, where certain Wind Turbine Operation data are collected and their Start and Stop can be controlled.

- The **Production Measurement System**, located in the Electrical Substation.

- The **Meteorological Station**.

- Other **electrical safety and control systems**, such as those destined to the generated Reactive Power Control.

On many occasions, the Monitoring and Control System (SCADA) of a farm is connected to another SCADA located in a far location, to which other SCADAs of other farms are also connected, and where the Operation data of all of them are aggregated.

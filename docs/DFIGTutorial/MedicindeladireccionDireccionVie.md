# Wind direction measurement - description of a wind vane

The direction of the wind is detected with a wind vane. See the video below to understand how it works.

![](<lib/Veleta1.gif>)

The signal generated by the wind vane is a digital signal that presents the measure of the nacelle disorientation, that is, the difference between the nacelle orientation and the wind direction. It is an oscillating signal whose "duty cycle" is the measure of this angle difference. The "work cycle" of a periodic signal is the relationship between the time the signal is in state "On" regarding the period of that signal.

The wind vane is a device that behaves like a pendulum excited by the wind rather than by gravity. As can be seen in the previous video, the photoelectric detector receives a signal when the path of its light is not hindered by the semicircle associated with the wind vane, which depends on the oscillation point of the same and its relative position regarding its base, which is fixed to the Nacelle. The following graph shows 4 situations that illustrate these concepts.

![](<lib/Situaciones Veleta.png>)

The opaque semicircle of the wind vane is represented by the pink semicircle. The circular sector indicates the oscillation area of the mobile part of the wind vane. In the first case (on the left) the position of the light source on the wind vane is never hindered: therefore the output of the wind vane sensor is always excited (black line). In the second case, the oscillation of the wind vane makes for a small part of the oscillation the light of the wind vane does not reach the sensor, and we have an output signal that, in most of the time, is excited. In the third one only during a small part of the oscillation cycle the sensor receives light. In the fourth case, the sensor does not receive light at any time from the oscillation.

The "disorientation" of the Nacelle to the wind is given by the difference between the middle angle on which the wind vane, which is that of the wind, oscillates and the direction of the Nacelle (the horizontal in the figure).

As can be seen when the sign of the wind vane is always on or off, the disorientation is greater than the angle of the wind vane oscillation (that is, it is large). When this signal oscillates between On and Off, its "look relationship" (duty cycle) allows for precise measurement of disorientation. When the look ratio is 50%, the difference is 0 °.

The measure of device disorientation requires a circuit that makes a statistical treatment of these three situations (always on, always on Off and oscillating), and, if it oscillates, to make a measure of their relationship of appearance.

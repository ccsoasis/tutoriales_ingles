# DFIG Generator

## Synoptic of the Electric Generator and the Back-to-Back converters.

This synoptic has several objectives:

1. To present the connectivity scheme of the Generator with the converters and with the grid.

2. To indicate at all times the power flow in the converters.

3. To present in real time the movement of the magnetic fluxes in the generator.

4. To present the calculations made in real time of the Generator and converter variables.

**Initial situation**

![](<lib/SinopticoDFIGB2B eatado inicial espanol.jpg>)

In this situation, the GSC remains connected to the grid and acts as a rectifier in charge of maintaining the voltage in the DC Link at an adequate level awaiting a later evolution. The power consumption from the grid is only the one derived from the losses that may exist in the DC Link and those necessary to keep the GSC in operation (i.e. very low).

The Generator indicator (top left) will show only the vector corresponding to the rotation of the Generator rotor, but reflected in the Power Train (i.e. reflected at the blade level). Therefore its speed will be between 10 and 20 rpms: that is, between 10/60 (=0.16) and 20/60 (=0.33) revolutions per second.

**Start of start-up (cutin)**

![](<lib/SinopticoDFIGB2B comienzo cutin espanol.jpg>)

When the Controller decides to start the start-up operation (cutin), the RSC (typically) becomes active but without connection to the rotor: it remains monitoring the signal that arrives from the Power Train angular position meter in order to know the frequency at which it will have to generate power when it connects to the rotor.

**Start-up: rotor connection**

In this step (there is still no production because the stator is not connected to the grid), the RSC connects to the rotor and generates an excitation voltage that serves to create a magnetic flux. Said flux induces a voltage in the stator (which is still disconnected from the grid), in such a way that its value and phase coincide with those of the grid. In this way, when the stator contactor closes, no transient will be generated in the grid.

![](<lib/SinopticoDFIGB2B conexion rotor espanol.jpg>)

This state is entered when the Power Train rpms are such that the frequency that the RSC must generate to excite the rotor is within its operating margin. The lower limit of this frequency corresponds -typically- to 20% to 25% of the rotation frequency of the flux vectors (that of the grid divided by the number of Generator poles), that is, in our case, between 5 Hz and 6.25 Hz.

The Generator indicator will now show three rotating vectors:

- The one corresponding to the mechanical rotation of the rotor shaft, in dark blue on a gray circle, which will rotate with the Power Train rpms, as before.

- The one corresponding to the magnetic flux generated by the excitation voltage forced by the RSC <u>*if the rotor shaft were not rotating*</u>, in green. Its rotation speed will correspond to the frequency of the signals created by the RSC, which will be the one the Power Train lacks to rotate at the Generator synchronous speed.

- The one corresponding to the <u>*real*</u> magnetic flux, in red. This vector rotates at a speed that is the sum of the Power Train rotation speed plus the rotation speed of the voltage imposed by the RSC. When the stator is connected to the grid it will produce an additional magnetic flux that will rotate at the same speed.

**Production case with rotor speed higher than the synchronous speed.**

The *Back-to-Back Controller* closes the stator contactor when the Power Train rpms are very close to those that the *Power Train Controller* decides are adequate for the existing wind. When that happens, as described in the previous point, the initial stator current is 0 (same as the power) and it grows at a rate that is also controllable. This growth of this power contributes to braking the Power Train acceleration, so that when its value reaches the value of the mechanical power taken from the wind, an equilibrium point is reached in which the Power Train remains stable.

When the rpms at which this equilibrium point occurs are higher than the synchronous ones, the synoptic will present a graph similar to the one in the following figure.

![](<lib/SinopticoDFIGB2B en produccion supersincrona.jpg>)

In this case, the green vector (the one created by the RSC voltage) will rotate in the opposite direction to the rotation direction of the rotor shaft. This is reflected as a negative frequency value.

**Production case with rotor speed lower than the synchronous speed.**

This case is similar to the previous one, but with a Power Train rotation speed lower than the synchronous one.

![](<lib/SinopticoDFIGB2B en produccion subsincrona es.jpg>)

In this case the green vector will rotate in the same direction as the Power Train. This is reflected in the positive sign of the frequency.

**About the numerical value indicators**  
*Background colors*:  
   
<mark>In white</mark>   voltages in volts.  
<mark>In red</mark>       powers in watts.  
<mark>In green</mark>     Generator efficiency.  
<mark>In blue</mark>       currents in amperes.  
<mark>In pink</mark>       frequency (in Hz) and Slip.  
<mark>In yellow</mark>  magnetic fluxes.  
   
The sign (-) in the power means that it "comes out" of the referenced element.  
   
*Value presentation formats*:  
   
Clicking with the left button on the indicator label changes the format from "Real/Imag" (real and imaginary parts) to "Mod/Ang" (modulus and angle).  
In "Mod/Ang" mode the angle can be changed between radians "R" and degrees "D".

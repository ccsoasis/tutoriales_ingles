# Wind direction measurement - description of a wind vane

The wind direction is detected with a **wind vane**. There are several wind vane models in relation to the measurement they give and the way of giving it. Some give an analog signal (generally a voltage between 0 and 10 V) that is proportional to the angle with respect to a given reference. Others incorporate an angular encoder with a communications line through which they give that angle measurement directly in numerical form.

Other wind vanes have a simpler structure and provide a digital signal whose "duty cycle" corresponds to the "orientation error" between a reference, given by its base which is fixed, and the wind vane itself. The following video shows its operation.

![](<lib/Veletaswf.gif>)

The mobile part ends in a non-complete disc, with a solid angle of less than 180 degrees. Let us say (180 -5) degrees. This disc interrupts or lets pass the light emitted by the LED of a device consisting of an LED and a photo-detector. The output of this photo-detector is the one shown in the oscilloscope of the previous figure.

The signal generated by the wind vane is a digital signal that presents the nacelle misorientation measurement, that is, the difference between the nacelle orientation and the wind direction. It is an oscillating signal whose "duty cycle" is the measurement of this angle difference. The "duty cycle" of a periodic signal is the relationship between the time the signal is in "On" state with respect to the period of said signal.

The wind vane is a device that behaves like a pendulum excited by the wind instead of by gravity. As can be seen in the previous video, the photoelectric detector receives signal when its light path is not obstructed by the semicircle associated with the wind vane, which depends on its oscillation point and its relative position with respect to its base, which is fixed to the nacelle. The following graph shows 4 situations that illustrate these concepts.

![](<lib/Situaciones Veleta.png>)

The opaque semicircle of the wind vane is represented by the pink semicircle. The circular sector indicates the oscillation zone of the wind vane mobile part. In the first case (on the left) the light source position in the wind vane is never obstructed: therefore the wind vane sensor output is always excited (black line). In the second case, the wind vane oscillation makes that during a small part of the oscillation the wind vane light does not reach the sensor, and we have an output signal that, most of the time, is excited. In the third, only during a small part of the oscillation cycle the sensor receives light. In the fourth case, the sensor does not receive light at any moment of the oscillation.

The nacelle "misorientation" with respect to the wind is given by the difference between the mean angle on which the wind vane oscillates, which is the wind one, and the nacelle direction (the horizontal in the figure).

As seen, when the wind vane signal is always On or Off, the misorientation is greater than the wind vane oscillation angle (that is, it is large). When this signal oscillates between On and Off, its "aspect ratio" (*duty cycle* in English) allows accurately measuring the misorientation. When the aspect ratio is 50%, the difference is 0º.

The misorientation measurement through the device requires a circuit that makes a statistical treatment of these three situations (always On, always Off and oscillating), and, in the case that it oscillates, that makes a measurement of its aspect ratio.

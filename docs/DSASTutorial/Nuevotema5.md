# Installation of a wind turbine

Wind turbines are classified into different **wind classes** based on the maximum wind speeds they're designed to withstand. This classification system, defined by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standard 61400-1, helps match turbines to appropriate installation sites.

### IEC Wind Classes

The main classification system uses four classes (I through IV), based on two key parameters:

**Class I** - Highest wind speeds

- Average wind speed: 10 m/s (22 mph)

- Extreme 50-year gust: 70 m/s (157 mph)

- Designed for very high wind sites like exposed coastal areas

**Class II** - High wind speeds

- Average wind speed: 8.5 m/s (19 mph)

- Extreme 50-year gust: 59.5 m/s (133 mph)

- Suitable for moderately exposed sites

**Class III** - Medium wind speeds

- Average wind speed: 7.5 m/s (17 mph)

- Extreme 50-year gust: 52.5 m/s (117 mph)

- Used for inland or less exposed locations

**Class IV** - Low wind speeds

- Average wind speed: 6 m/s (13 mph)

- Extreme 50-year gust: 42 m/s (94 mph)

- Designed for low-wind inland sites

There's also a **Class S** (Special) category for turbines with non-standard design parameters specified by the designer.

### Operational Wind Speed Ranges

Additionally, turbines have three critical operational thresholds:

- **Cut-in speed**: Minimum wind speed to start generating power (typically 3-4 m/s)

- **Rated speed**: Wind speed at which maximum power output is reached (typically 12-16 m/s)

- **Cut-out speed**: Maximum operational wind speed before the turbine shuts down for safety (typically 25 m/s or about 56 mph)

The cut-out speed protects the turbine from damage during storms, which is separate from the extreme wind speeds the structure must survive when shut down.

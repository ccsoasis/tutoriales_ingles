# Gearboxes

The purpose of the gearbox is to transmit the mechanical torque created by the Blades connected to the Hub to the electric Generator shaft, adapting it to the rpms required by it.

The blades rotate at a low regime, between 10 rpms and 45 rpms (depending on models and powers), while the electric Generator rotates between 1000 rpms and 2000 rpms, also depending on models and powers.

The following figure shows a transverse section of a gearbox: in this case, it contains three stages, the first of them of the "planetary" type.

![](<lib/gearbox-failure-fig1.jpg>)

Fig 1.

The input (on the left) is done through a device (Planet carrier in green) that transmits the power to the planets (in red). This device is supported by the bearings (*Carrier bearings*), which are on the casing. The planets are geared, on one side to the *Ring gear* (toothed crown) which is fixed to the casing and therefore does not rotate, and on the other moves the Sun (*Sun pinion*), on the Intermediate stage shaft (*Intermediate gear* in blue). The other end of this stage moves the next stage gear, which moves the output pinion (*High speed pinion*). The dark blue zones correspond to sections of the bearings in which the different rotating elements are supported.

In the previous figure, there are three planets; the following figure shows a more simplified view, but with 4 planets.

![](<lib/Esquema multiplicadora AeroTorque.jpg>)

Fig 2. (Courtesy of AeroTorque Inc.)

In this case the blade power enters from the right.

The fundamental advantage of the planet stages is the increase of the contact surface for power transmission, proportional to the planet number.

The gearbox ratio corresponds to the product of the multiplication ratio of each stage. The values in wind turbines go from 1:50 to 1:120 in large wind turbines.

Another image that allows seeing the simplified structure of a typical wind turbine gearbox is the following. In the left part the left lateral view is presented, cut at the "AA" plane level.

![](<lib/Gearbox_structure.png>)

The power input (the Hub connection) is done from the left (*Rotor shaft*), and the output (generator connection) is done from the right part (*High Speed Shaft*). The rotor shaft rotation moves the three planetary pinions: since the "annulus" is fixed to the structure and therefore does not move, that movement makes those three pinions move in the indicated circumference, and therefore move the planetary central pinion, which is attached to the toothed wheel that meshes with the second stage (*Middle Shaft*).

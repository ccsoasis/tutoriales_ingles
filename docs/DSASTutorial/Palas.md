# Blade angle change mechanism (Pitch)

The blade angle (pitch) is used to:

- **Control the power taken from the air and transmitted to the Power Train**. In production, this power is equal to the power delivered to the Electrical Grid, minus the power dissipated in friction and other losses.

- **Stop the Power Train aerodynamically**. The brake system is used only to *fix* the Power Train, once stopped, or as a safety mechanism in case it could not be stopped aerodynamically.

Additionally, and in modern wind turbines, the angle of each blade can be modified independently per blade. The reasons for having independent control for each blade is to be able to compensate the wind loss that occurs when one of the blades passes in front of the Tower, and also to compensate the wind difference that exists along the Tower and up to the highest position the blades can reach.

This wind difference in the vertical direction is called ***wind shear***, and depends on the wind turbine location, the surrounding terrain roughness, the nearby building, the wind direction, etc. An example of the vertical wind distribution is shown in the following figure.

In wind turbines where the blade length (and therefore the Tower height) is large, the mechanical stress difference along them is significant.

The passage in front of the Tower translates, if there is no pitch angle compensation, into ***flicker*** in the produced electrical power. This is a parameter that is highly taken into account in relation to the ***Power Quality***.

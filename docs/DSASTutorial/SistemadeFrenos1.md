## Functions and elements of the yaw mechanism

The nacelle yawing mechanism towards the wind (***up wind*** in English) has the following functionalities:

- ***The nacelle orientation***.  
For this it has two sets of **yaw motors**, one for each direction, which are geared to a toothed crown, which is fixed to the upper part of the Tower. These motors are controlled from the Controller, which implements the adequate algorithms. The rotation directions are called in English ***CW*** (*clockwise*, in the direction of the clock hands movement) and ***CCW*** (*counter clockwise*, in the opposite direction).  
In the maintenance of this orientation one must take into account:

- ○The nacelle orientation must be such that the wind direction is perpendicular to the blade rotation plane, *most of the time, and with a minimum error*.

- ○The energy necessary to rotate the nacelle, and the blades that are attached to it.

- ○The existing wind: if it is too low it may not be important to guarantee that the nacelle is oriented. These algorithms have to do with:

- ○Keeping the nacelle orientation so that the wind direction is perpendicular to the blade rotation plane, most of the time.

- ○Preventing the existing misorientation, as well as the

- ***Keeping the nacelle with a fixed orientation***.  
This is achieved with some **brake shoes** that fix the nacelle to the Tower. These brakes are

- ***Detecting and controlling the number of power cable turns*** that go down to the Tower base from the Generator. These cables fall freely for a few meters until they are finally fixed to the Tower wall. The (limited) flexibility of these cables is used to solve the (power) connection problem between a fixed part (the electrical cabinet, at the Tower base) and a mobile one (the Generator, which rotates with the nacelle.)
